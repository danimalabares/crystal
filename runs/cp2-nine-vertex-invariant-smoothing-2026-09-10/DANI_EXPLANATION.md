## 1. The object

`S = ℚ[x₁,…,x₉]`, `I = I_Δ` is generated by the 36 quartic monomials `x_a x_b x_c x_d` over the 36 four-element
non-faces of the nine-vertex triangulation Δ of ℂP². `A = S/I`, `X = Proj A ⊂ ℙ⁸` is the union of the 36 coordinate
ℙ⁴'s (one per facet). The 36 generators have exactly 90 linear syzygies (the 36×90 matrix `R₀`), i.e. the relations
`Σ_j (F₀)_j (R₀)_{jk} = 0`, and these generate all relations.

`H = Aut(Δ)` has order 54 and permutes the variables. It is `F₃² ⋊ S₃`: the nine vertices form the affine plane
`F₃²`, the normal subgroup `F₃²` acts by translations, and the stabiliser of a vertex is `S₃`. It has two orbits on the
36 generators: an orbit of 9 (representative `x₁x₂x₃x₄`, the three "rows" `{123},{456},{789}` each times one point of
the next row) and an orbit of 27 (representative `x₁x₂x₄x₈`).

Numbers carried over from the 2026-09-09 run and re-verified today (byte-identical outputs): `dim Hom_S(I,A)₀ = 93`,
coordinate changes give 72 of them, so 21 intrinsic first-order deformations; `dim (T²_A)₀ = 126`; `H`-invariants:
5 in `Hom_S(I,A)₀`, of which 3 are coordinate changes, and 0 in `(T²_A)₀`.

## 2. First-order deformations and the two invariant directions

A first-order deformation of the presentation is a row `F₁` of 36 quartics such that `F₀ + t F₁` still has 90
relations modulo `t²`, i.e. `F₁ R₀ ≡ 0 (mod I)`: the map `I → A`, `(F₀)_j ↦ (F₁)_j` kills the syzygies. That is
`Hom_S(I,A)₀`. Coordinate changes `x_i ↦ x_i + t x_j` produce 72 dimensions of these; the remaining 21 are genuine.

The invariant ones. `H` acts on such rows by `F ↦ g(F)·P_gᵀ` where `P_g` is the permutation of the 36 generators
induced by `g` (so that `F₀` itself is fixed). Averaging the 93 basis vectors over the 54 group elements and taking the
span gives the 5 invariant directions; three of them are the invariant coordinate changes (the centraliser of `H` in
`GL₉` is a 4-dimensional commutative algebra, spanned by the four orbital matrices; modulo scalars a 3-dimensional
torus). The two remaining directions, written out completely in `certificates/lift2_tangents.m2.txt`, are:

- `v_a`, supported on the 9-orbit: `x₁x₂x₃·x_p ↦ (1/3) x_p·x₄x₅x₆` for `p ∈ {4,5,6}`, `x₄x₅x₆·x_p ↦ (1/3) x_p·x₇x₈x₉`
  for `p ∈ {7,8,9}`, `x₇x₈x₉·x_p ↦ (1/3) x_p·x₁x₂x₃` for `p ∈ {1,2,3}`, and `0` on the 27-orbit. To first order the
  nine generators become `x_p·(x₁x₂x₃ + (t/3)x₄x₅x₆)` etc.: each factors as a variable times a cubic.
- `v_b`, supported on the 27-orbit: `x₁x₂x₄x₈ ↦ (1/18)(x₁²x₆x₇ + x₂²x₅x₉)` and its 26 `H`-translates, `0` on the
  9-orbit.

These two are exactly what "2-dimensional intrinsic invariant tangent space" means: two rows of quartics, each killing
the 90 syzygies modulo `I`, each mapped to itself by all 54 permutations, and independent modulo coordinate changes.

## 3. Invariant ideal versus invariant polynomials

`I` is `H`-invariant as an ideal, but the `H`-invariant quartics inside `I₄` form only a 2-dimensional space (the two
orbit sums), which generates a much smaller ideal. Invariance of an ideal means the *set* of generators is permuted, not
that each generator is fixed. The right formulation for a family is therefore: a row `F(t)` of 36 polynomials with
`g(F(t)) = F(t)·P_g` for all `g ∈ H` (generators permuted among themselves, in the same pattern as for `F₀`) and a
relation matrix `R(t)` with `g(R(t))` equal to `P_g⁻¹ R(t) C_g` for the constant matrices `C_g` defined by
`R₀ C_g = P_g g(R₀)`. Every `F_n`, `R_n` computed today satisfies these identities exactly (checked for all 54 elements).

Likewise "an invariant class in `Hom_S(I,A)₀`" is a class fixed by `H`; its representatives are fixed only up to
elements of `I`. We chose the canonical normal-form representative modulo the monomial ideal `I`, which *is* fixed.

## 4. Lifting order by order and why the obstruction vanishes

Write `F = F₀ + F₁ + F₂ + …`, `R = R₀ + R₁ + R₂ + …` with `F_n`, `R_n` homogeneous of degree `n` in `t = (t₁,t₂)`,
with `F₁ = t₁ v_a + t₂ v_b`. Flatness modulo `(t)^{N+1}` is the single matrix identity `F·R ≡ 0 (mod (t)^{N+1})`
(every relation lifts). At order `n` this reads

    F₀ R_n + F_n R₀ = −(F₁R_{n−1} + … + F_{n−1}R₁) =: −ρ_n.

The row `ρ_n` reduced modulo `I` is a homomorphism on the relations that kills the second syzygies and the Koszul
relations; its class in `(T²_A)₀ = Hom(R/Koszul, A)/(trivial ones)` is *the* obstruction. If the class is zero,
`ρ_n ≡ F_n R₀ (mod I)` is solvable for `F_n`, and then `F₀ R_n = −ρ_n − F_n R₀` is solvable for `R_n` because the
right-hand side lies in `I` entrywise. If all lower orders are `H`-invariant, `ρ_n` is `H`-invariant, hence so is its
class, hence the class lies in `(T²)^H = 0`. The solution set `{(F_n, R_n)}` is an affine space stable under `H`, so
averaging any solution over the 54 elements gives an invariant solution. This is the whole content of the equivariant
lifting lemma; it needs `|H| = 54` invertible and nothing else.

Two parameters. Passing from `ℚ[t₁,t₂]/(t)^{n+1}` to `ℚ[t₁,t₂]/(t)^{n+2}` is a small extension whose kernel
`(t)^{n+1}/(t)^{n+2}` is a vector space of dimension `n+2` with trivial `H`-action; the obstruction is an element of
`T² ⊗ (kernel)`, invariant, hence zero, and the lifts form a torsor under `T¹ ⊗ (kernel)`, on which averaging picks an
invariant lift. Hence a formal invariant two-parameter family over `ℚ[[t₁,t₂]]` exists, and every finite truncation of it
can be taken to be the one computed here.

What was actually computed: the obstruction classes at orders 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 were found to vanish explicitly (not just by
the lemma), and the invariant `F_n`, `R_n` are stored (`certificates/lift6_state_order6.m2.txt`, verified by
`scripts/verify_lift_state.py` in independent exact arithmetic; `logs/verify_lift_state_order6.log`). Noteworthy: `R₂ = 0` exactly, and `F₃`, `F₄`, `F₅`, `F₆` are
supported on the 9-orbit only. `F·R` is not identically zero, so the family is not polynomial in this gauge.

## 5. From graded deformations of `A` to the Hilbert scheme, and back

`A` is not Cohen–Macaulay, but `depth A = 3`: `H¹_m(A) = H²_m(A) = 0` (verified by `Ext` duality and by
`h¹(X, O_X(d)) = 0` for all tested `d`). Two consequences were checked, not assumed. First, `Hom_S(I,A)₀ = H⁰(N_X)`, the
tangent space of `Hilb(ℙ⁸)` at `[X]`; the sheaf-cohomology computation gives 93 as well. Second, because
`H¹(O_X(d)) = 0` for all `d`, every embedded deformation of `X` over an Artinian base comes from a graded deformation of
`A` (take `⊕_d H⁰(O(d))`) and conversely (take `Proj`), so the two deformation problems coincide, including obstruction
spaces. Therefore `(T²_A)₀ = 126` is a complete obstruction space for the Hilbert scheme at `[X]`, and the `H`-fixed
locus of the Hilbert scheme is **smooth of dimension 5** at `[X]`; the torus orbit accounts for 3 dimensions, leaving a
2-dimensional family of genuinely different `H`-invariant subschemes. This settles equivariant algebraization: the
formal family lives in an honest smooth 5-dimensional germ of `H`-invariant projective schemes.

## 6. Smoothability of the invariant family **[verdict]**

- Structure of `X`: at each of the nine coordinate points `X` is locally the affine cone over the Grünbaum
  eight-vertex sphere threefold `X_M ⊂ ℙ⁷` (the vertex link is isomorphic to `M`, and the vertex stabiliser `S₃` is the
  packet's group `G`). Along the open ℙ³'s two components cross normally; along the 84 triangles the transverse model is
  the cone over a triangulated circle (`xyz = 0` for the 3-cycles).
- Fixed points of the translation `g = (123)(456)(789)`: on `X` they are nine triple points (local model `b³ = 0`). On the
  invariant family along `t₁ = t₂ = q` the local ideal is exactly `(a, 3b³ + q)` modulo `q⁵`, so the triple points split
  into reduced points at order one. A smooth invariant fibre must have smooth fixed loci; this necessary condition holds.
- **Jacobian certificate on the chart `x₉ = 1`** (sufficient for all nine charts by vertex-transitivity): the finite-jet
  criterion `q^e ∈ (f, Jac·λ) + (q^{e+1})` was set up with an auxiliary vector `λ` (rank of the Jacobian < 4 iff some
  `λ ≠ 0` kills four random combinations of the equations) but the Gröbner basis at order 4 modulo 32003 did not finish
  in 17 hours and was killed. **Verdict: unresolved.** No invariant fibre is known to be smooth and none is known to be
  singular. (A mod-`p` certificate would also have needed the observation that all coefficients so far lie in `ℤ[1/6]`,
  so that the family is defined over `ℤ_(p)[[q]]` and generic smoothness passes from `𝔽_p((q))` to `ℚ((q))`.)
- The most promising route is visible in the jets themselves: with `s = t₁/3`, `u = t₂/18` the cubics `C_i` and the
  27-orbit quartics have coefficients that look like closed-form series (Hesse-pencil cubics `x₁x₂x₃`, `Σx³`). A few
  more orders should let one guess exact algebraic equations, verify `F·R = 0` exactly, and test a single fibre.

## 7. What the numbers say about Galkin's Kummer suggestion (consistency only)

The Hilbert polynomial of `A` is `(3/2)m⁴ + (9/2)m² + 3`, exactly Riemann–Roch for a generalized Kummer fourfold
with a polarisation of Beauville–Bogomolov square 2 (`χ(mH) = 3·binom(m²+2, 2)`), and `h²(O_X) = h⁴(O_X) = 1`,
`h¹ = h³ = 0`, `χ(O_X) = 3`. This is the numerical shadow of a Kum₂ degeneration, not evidence that a smoothing exists
or is of that type.
